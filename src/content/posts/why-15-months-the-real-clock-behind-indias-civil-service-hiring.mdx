<section id="why-the-clock-runs-slow">
      <h2 class="section-head">Why the Clock Runs Slow?</h2>
<p>Have you ever wondered why a civil service job in India feels like a marathon? The answer lies in the exam’s structure. The UPSC doesn’t just test knowledge; it tests endurance, depth, and adaptability.</p>
<p>The exam is split into three parts: a preliminary paper, a main exam, and an interview. Each part is scheduled months apart. That alone stretches the process.</p>
<p>But there’s more. The selection committee reviews thousands of papers. They must sift through essays, answer sheets, and interview recordings. That’s a slow, meticulous process.</p>
<p>In short, the 15‑month timeline isn’t accidental. It’s a built‑in filter that weeds out the weak and hones the strong.</p>
    </section>
    <section id="the-exam-calendar-a-year-in-the-life-of-a-candidate">
      <h2 class="section-head">The Exam Calendar: A Year in the Life of a Candidate</h2>
<p>The UPSC exam calendar is a tightrope walk.</p>
<p>Prelims usually happen in January. Candidates who clear prelims move to mains in March or April. The main exam can take up to a week, with 12 papers.</p>
<p>Results for prelims come in February. Mains results are announced in May. The interview is scheduled for June or July.</p>
<p>That’s about six months from the first exam to the final interview. After that, the selection committee takes a few weeks to finalize the list.</p>
<p>So, from the first paper to the interview, you’re already halfway through the 15 months.</p>
    </section>
    <section id="beyond-the-paper-why-training-takes-half-a-year">
      <h2 class="section-head">Beyond the Paper: Why Training Takes Half a Year</h2>
<p>Once selected, the journey doesn’t end. The Civil Services Training Institute (CSTI) in Mussoorie starts in September.</p>
<p>New officers undergo a 12‑month program that covers governance, public policy, and administrative skills.</p>
<p>The training is intensive. It includes classroom learning, field visits, and role‑play scenarios. The goal is to turn a fresh graduate into a competent administrator.</p>
<p>Because training is so thorough, it adds a full year to the process.</p>
    </section>
    <section id="the-hidden-gatekeepers-bureaucracy-vs-merit">
      <h2 class="section-head">The Hidden Gatekeepers: Bureaucracy vs Merit</h2>
<p>The UPSC’s longevity is partly due to its gatekeepers.</p>
<p>The selection committee is a panel of senior civil servants. They’re trained to spot nuance, consistency, and potential.</p>
<p>Their job is not to rush. They cross‑check answers, look for logical gaps, and assess interview performance against a set of competencies.</p>
<p>This scrutiny keeps the process slow but ensures that the few who pass are truly ready for the challenges of public service.</p>
<p>Thus, the 15‑month timeline reflects a system that prioritises merit over speed.</p>
    </section>
